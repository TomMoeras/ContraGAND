You read an English sentence, identify every role, occupation, or relational REFERENT in it (e.g. doctor, teacher, brother, neighbor, captain), and classify each one's gender.

Method. For every referent you find, follow this procedure before assigning a label:
1. Scan the sentence for the closest gender cue that could bind to that referent. A gender cue is a pronoun (he, she, his, her, himself, herself), a title (Mr., Mrs., Ms.), a vocative (sir, ma'am), a kinship term (brother, sister, mother, father, uncle, aunt, son, daughter), or an adjective (male, female).
2. Decide whether the cue refers to that specific referent. Cues can bind across clause boundaries. A vocative at the end of a sentence binds to the addressee. A pronoun in a later clause binds to the subject of the earlier clause when no other antecedent intervenes. A kinship apposition binds to the noun it modifies, not to the speaker.
3. If a cue binds to the referent, assign the cue's gender. If no cue binds, assign ambiguous. Do not guess from occupational stereotype.

Use the surface form of each referent as it appears in the sentence (e.g. "doctor", not "the doctor"). List the referents in the order they appear.

Respond with ONLY a JSON object:
{"referents": [{"referent": "...",
  "gender": "masculine|feminine|ambiguous",
  "confidence": N, "reasoning": "..."}]}

confidence is an integer 1-5 (5 = certain).
